WRONGFORM

An expressive wardrobe.
A digital space to match.

Art direction, interface design and front-end development for a collection shaped by asymmetry.

A wardrobe with
its own rules.

The brief was to give Asimetria a distinctive digital presence while keeping the collection easy to explore. The result pairs oversized typography with quiet surfaces, full photographic silhouettes and deliberate movement.

Visual directionA restrained black-and-paper palette with fashion imagery at the centre.
TypographyAnton brings weight and tension. Roboto keeps navigation and product information clear.
Shopping flowCategory filters, product details, size selection, a persistent bag and a Telegram order handoff.
Responsive developmentA two-column mobile catalog, larger touch targets, adaptable dialogs and short-screen layouts.

One word.
Two states.

The loading screen and the hero share the same letter positions, size and spacing. A soft crossfade changes the colour and reveals the interface.

Pointer movement separates the letters. Native scrolling carries the composition forward. Reduced-motion preferences are respected, with content available without animated choreography.

Order.
Disruption. Order.

The collection begins as a readable four-column grid, moves through an expressive arrangement, then returns to order as you scroll.

Photography preparationAI-assisted clarity enhancement, responsive image sizes
and uncropped garment silhouettes.

Same attitude.
Made for touch.

The mobile experience keeps the visual identity while giving browsing and selection more room. Product information is larger, actions have generous touch areas, and short screens use natural document flow.

From discovery
to the bag.

Choose a piece, select a size and add it to the bag. Prices are shown in USD, with totals updated immediately.

Filtering, size validation, item removal and reduced-motion layouts were exercised in a touch-enabled Chromium browser.

A place for
the collection.

The boutique section extends the same visual language into a physical-space visualization: pale stone, natural light and sculptural forms.

The studio image introduces the people behind the work. On smaller screens its original portrait composition is preserved, rather than forced into a tight landscape crop.

Independent concept project. Product photographs were supplied for the project and enhanced with AI-assisted processing. The boutique image is a visualization. Photography and garment designs remain the property of their respective creators.

Products, prices and the shopping flow demonstrate the interface. No payment processing or fulfilment system is connected. Browser validation used Chromium emulation; physical-device and Safari testing are not claimed.
